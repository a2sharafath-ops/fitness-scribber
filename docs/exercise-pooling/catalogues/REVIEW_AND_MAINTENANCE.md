# Content review, publication and maintenance specification

U03 / O04 / Q03 · v0.2 · proposed workflow, not an implemented admin console.

## Review responsibility

The fitness department head is the recommended coordinator for ordinary exercise/task/goal/dose review, subject to relevant competence. The owner accepts product priorities. A suitably licensed clinician reviews clinical policy and the exact rehabilitation population/jurisdiction within their competence. Privacy/legal counsel reviews regional processing/service duties; rights reviewer verifies reusable content. A title, generic qualification, owner approval or AI-authored draft is not substitute evidence of the required scope.

One person may fill compatible roles if authority is documented. The single operational coach account does not imply all review disciplines must be the same person or that external reviewers receive client data. This preparation review needs synthetic data only.

## Lifecycle and authoring UI

States: draft → validation-ready → in-review → changes-requested or accepted-for-scope → included-in-published-manifest → retired or revoked. Drafts can be rejected without deleting their review trail. These are proposed future content states; today's records remain draft.

Screen sections, in order: identity/revision; intended population/task; role/pattern/need; prerequisites and access; equipment/position/demands; instructions/side/dose; alternatives; sources/rights; expected cases; reviewer decisions; publication history. Editing a reviewed field creates a new revision and identifies affected dependencies. Required fields have explicit unknown states, not fabricated defaults.

Actions: save draft (no admission); validate metadata (report errors); request review (export bundle, no automatic external message); record evidenced review (scope and conditions); create proposed manifest (no live enablement); retire/revoke (future-use impact preview and authorization). Production publishing is a privileged later action with scoped release authorization, not a client or ordinary draft-save permission.

Review queue filters by missing source, rights, clinical scope, unsupported coverage, affected published dependencies and overdue review trigger. Use exact IDs/revisions, not name matching. Show original versus proposed fields and which tests/clients' future assignments could be affected, with authorized minimal projections.

## Per-record acceptance checklist

1. Is this the exact variant, position, equipment and population being reviewed? Are instructions original/licensed and usable without an inaccessible video?
2. Are prerequisites, range, impact, assistance and relevant restrictions complete for that scope? What is explicitly outside scope?
3. Are target/observed/execution side and dose basis compatible? Are source measurements and load assumptions legitimate?
4. Does the mapping state a confirmed task need rather than a muscle diagnosis? Are deduplication and resolution rules defensible?
5. Is the proposed dose complete, individualized and within approved limits? Is every rest/setup/transfer included in time examples?
6. Do alternatives preserve required need and constraints? Is an unsupported gap preferable to the proposed substitution?
7. Do positive, negative, missing/conflicting-data and access cases have agreed expected results?
8. Is usage-rights evidence sufficient for the exact text/instrument/media/version/channel? Does a source support the actual claim?

Decision evidence: reviewer identity and verified role/scope/jurisdiction, artifact ID/revision/hash, decision, rationale, required amendments, accepted population/settings, exclusions, source evidence, date and review/change triggers. Store signatures only when genuinely supplied. “Reviewed” without these fields is not admission.

## Publication, retirement and incidents

A proposed manifest pins exact exercise, mapping, dose, reason, protocol and policy revisions plus all necessary reviews and rights decisions. The admission check rejects missing or revoked dependencies. Clinical candidates additionally need an accepted module scope; client-specific instructions never approve the global catalogue. Publishing cannot exceed the release's country/population/role boundaries.

Routine retirement removes a variant from new selection but preserves historical content and completed actuals. A clinically consequential or rights revocation must invalidate affected future use and trigger re-review according to the accepted policy. Do not mutate an old prescription's exercise name, dose or source in place. Display a replacement proposal with reason; require approval of its new revision.

Maintenance triggers: source/instrument or rights change; reviewer withdrawal/expiry; reported content issue; failed expected case; changed scope; incident; new equipment/translation; provider/regulatory change. Owner assigns actual review intervals before launch; there is no invented universal annual expiry. Keep a change log and downstream dependency impact report. Reports of injury or symptoms go through the clinical/incident process, not an automatic content rewrite.

A paused feature must preserve access to stop/report, actuals and relevant support/privacy functions. Re-enablement needs the authorized release owner and required reviewers, not just a green metadata check.
