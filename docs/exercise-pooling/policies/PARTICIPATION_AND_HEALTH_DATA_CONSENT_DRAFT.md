## Part A — understanding participation

My coach will explain the proposed activity, purpose, practical demands, available alternatives and relevant limits before I agree. I may ask questions, decline an activity, request modification or stop. An activity outside the provider's competence or documented restrictions requires appropriate review rather than improvisation.

Physical activity involves risks depending on the person and activity. These can include discomfort, soreness, strains, falls or aggravation of an existing problem; more serious events may occur. The provider will explain material risks relevant to the proposed activity and required supervision. This general acknowledgement does not replace that discussion or an individual review.

The client app is not an emergency service and is not continuously monitored. If I develop unexpected symptoms or feel unwell during activity, I should stop and seek appropriate assistance. For an emergency, use local emergency services rather than waiting for an app reply. Service instructions will explain remote/in-person support arrangements.

Software may suggest exercises, but the coach must approve assignment. Coach approval is not medical clearance. For clinician-guided rehabilitation, the responsible professional, permitted activity and limits must be identified separately. This form does not authorize diagnosis, medical treatment changes or work outside professional scope.

- [ ] I understand coach-reviewed training is distinct from any separately required clinical authorization.

## Evidence and accessibility

Record actual client identity/action, purpose, version, timestamp, channel and withdrawal. The coach must not silently tick client-app choices. For consent obtained outside the app, retain legally acceptable evidence and its collection method. Give the client a copy and accessible support. A representative acts only within verified lawful authority; disability alone does not establish incapacity.

Nothing here asks the client to waive statutory rights or absolves the provider of duties that cannot lawfully be waived.
