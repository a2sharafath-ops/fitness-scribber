# Training load graph evidence review

Reviewed 28 September 2026 for the Classic client Progress workspace. These graphs describe recorded internal training load for one client. They are not injury prediction, medical clearance, or prescription rules.

## Literature and implications

| Evidence | Finding relevant to this workspace | Graph decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Foster et al., 2001](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/A%20New%20Approach%20to%20Monitoring%20Exercise%20Training.pdf) and a [session-RPE validity review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29163016/) | Session RPE multiplied by duration is a practical internal-load measure, though context affects perceived effort. | Plot values derived only from recorded session RPE × duration. Label load in arbitrary units and retain source records. |
| [Foster, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9662690/) | Training monotony is daily mean load divided by day-to-day standard deviation; strain combines load and monotony. The original work was a small observational athlete study, not a universal threshold validation. | Show the actual 7-day mean, standard deviation, sum, and monotony behind selected points. Do not add red/green bands. |
| [IOC load and injury consensus, 2016](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/50/17/1030) | Individual monitoring is useful, but no single marker consistently predicts maladaptation or injury. | Treat all three graphs as contextual exposure descriptions alongside the trainer's assessment, not clearance decisions. |
| [Impellizzeri et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/) and [Dalen-Lorentsen et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472501/) | ACWR interpretation is sensitive to ratio construction and analytical choices; evidence does not establish a causal safe zone. | Keep the existing coupled 7-day/28-day calculation explicit. Do not draw an injury-risk threshold. Display both component averages at the selected date. |
| [Ding et al., 2026 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42662491/) | Elevated ACWR had a small-to-moderate pooled association with injury in team sports, but heterogeneity was very high (I² 95.8%); the authors reject ACWR as a stand-alone causal or predictive model. | Retain ACWR as a contextual measure without a universal cutoff or automated training recommendation. |
| [Griffin et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33971793/) and [handling/reporting missing load data, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36412175/) | Missing session data can distort ACWR, monotony, and strain; missingness and its handling should be reported. | Plot only days with recorded session RPE and show observation counts. State that the legacy formulas use zero for unlogged days without claiming those days were rest. No values are imputed for display. |

## Implemented display rules

- The graph uses **calendar dates** on the horizontal axis. It shows points only on dates with a session RPE entry and a calculable measure; it does not interpolate through unlogged days. The trainer can choose a 28-, 56-, or 84-day **display period**. Those choices do not change the 7-day or 28-day calculation windows.
- Selecting a recorded date reveals the actual inputs: ACWR's 7- and 28-day daily means; monotony's 7-day daily mean and standard deviation; or strain's 7-day sum and monotony. The readout includes logged-day counts and source labels.
- The optional shaded **personal baseline band** is recalculated for each graph date as the 25th–75th percentile of that client's calculable values in the **preceding 28 calendar days**, excluding the graph date and every later value. It needs at least four earlier values to render and is drawn only from the first through last recorded date, never across an entirely unobserved leading or trailing period. Changing the graph's display period does not change the band's calculation on an individual date. This is a transparent descriptive UI comparison, **not** a validated target, uncertainty interval, or injury-risk boundary. The user can hide it.
- Each compact card compares its latest recent calculable value with that same dated personal band. It shows **↑ Above baseline**, **↓ Below baseline**, or **≈ Within baseline**. These describe position relative to the middle half of prior values, not clinical improvement or deterioration. If the latest result cannot be calculated, it shows **No comparison**; if there are fewer than four earlier calculable values, it shows **Baseline unavailable**. The card retains its observation date, while its accessible label and popup disclose the calculation reason, coverage, sources, and missingness.
- The existing formula for ACWR is a coupled 7-day daily mean divided by a 28-day daily mean; ACWR is withheld until at least three positive-load days exist in the 28-day window. Monotony and strain use the existing 7-day formulas. No new physiological or clinical rule was introduced.
- Each card opens its own graph and expandable dated table for exact values, window coverage, and sources. The graph's text summary and date selector provide an accessible alternative to pointer-only chart inspection.
- A card with no session RPE entry in the last 28 days says **Not available** instead of promoting an older value. A recorded date with no calculable result also says **Not available**; the mathematical reason is available in its accessible label and popup. The existing ACWR three-positive-day floor is disclosed as part of that calculation, not presented as a validated clinical minimum. Available values remain dated; the popup shows how many days have entries, while unlogged days are explicitly unconfirmed. The popup preserves recorded history and explains an empty graph without drawing zeros or a synthetic trend.

## Limits

The underlying records do not identify confirmed rest days separately from unlogged days. The formulas currently enter those days as zero, so a low observation count should prompt record review before interpretation. A personal percentile band from a short history can be unstable; adjacent rolling values are overlapping observations, not independent trials. The band must not be treated as a validated baseline or alert boundary. The literature does not justify a universal ACWR “sweet spot” or a monotony/strain cutoff for this mixed personal-training population.
